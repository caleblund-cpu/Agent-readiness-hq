5 practical ways to use AI in your small business this week

October 10, 2026

Most AI advice for small businesses is either vague ("embrace the future!") or built for companies with IT departments. Here are five things you can actually do this week, with tools you already have access to, each taking an afternoon or less.

1. Draft your customer emails in seconds

Quotes, follow-ups, "sorry we missed you" notes, review requests. Give the AI the facts (who, what, what happens next) and let it draft. You edit for tone and hit send. Most owners tell us this alone saves an hour a week.

2. Turn one idea into a week of content

Write down one thing you explained to a customer this week. Ask the AI to turn it into a short blog post, three social posts, and an email newsletter draft. One input, four outputs. Consistency is what grows an audience, and this removes the blank-page problem.

3. Write the FAQ page your customers (and AI agents) need

List the ten questions you answer over and over. Have the AI help you write clear, complete answers, then publish them as an FAQ page. This does double duty: it saves you from repeating yourself, and it gives AI agents structured facts about your business to quote when someone asks about you.

4. Summarize instead of re-reading

Long contract, supplier proposal, industry report, rambling meeting transcript. Paste it in and ask for the key points, the costs, and the deadlines. You still read the important parts yourself, but you stop reading everything twice.

5. Research competitors and vendors fast

Ask the AI to compare three competitors' pricing, or to pull together background on a vendor before a call. Treat it as a fast intern: great at gathering, but you verify anything that matters before acting on it.

Two ground rules

First, never paste private customer data (names, emails, payment details) into a public AI tool. Second, check its work on anything factual, especially prices, dates, and legal-sounding claims. AI is confident even when it is wrong. You are still the expert on your business.

Start with one. Email drafting is the easiest win for most owners. Once that feels normal, the rest follow naturally.
