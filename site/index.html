How the Agent Readiness HQ score works

One score, 0–100, built from seven machine-readability checks:

  1. robots.txt posture toward AI crawlers: whether your site permits the crawlers agents rely on.
  2. llms.txt (experimental): a proposed convention intended to provide AI-oriented information about a website. Adoption and support vary.
  3. Sitemap: whether agents can discover every page worth knowing about.
  4. Schema.org structured data: machine-readable facts about your business, products, and services.
  5. Machine-readable pricing: can an agent extract a price without guessing?
  6. Bot-walls & CAPTCHAs: friction that stops agents (and sometimes customers) cold.
  7. Page speed: slow pages time out agents the same way they bounce humans.

Frequently asked questions

What is an AI agent?

An AI agent is software that acts on someone's behalf: researching options, comparing prices, filling carts, even completing purchases. Think of it as a tireless personal assistant that does the shopping for the human. When your customer sends an agent instead of visiting your site themselves, the agent's experience is your customer's experience.

Will this hurt my SEO?

Many good machine-readability practices — such as clear site structure, valid structured data, accessible content and useful sitemaps — also overlap with established technical web and SEO practices. However, Agent Readiness HQ does not promise improved search rankings or traffic.

What if I block AI crawlers on purpose?

That can be a legitimate choice. AI crawlers and automated systems serve different purposes, and blocking one does not necessarily mean blocking all AI-related access. Our scan identifies relevant crawler-access settings so you can make an informed decision about which automated systems you choose to allow.
