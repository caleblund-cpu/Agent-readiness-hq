Your website platform's free AI tools: what they cover, what they miss

October 31, 2026 · By Agent Readiness HQ

If your website runs on a major platform, your inbox has probably seen the announcements. Kajabi's new AI Discoverability feature starts November 9 and promises to give AI assistants a clean view of your public pages and offers. Wix and GoDaddy advertise built-in AI visibility scores. Shopify has quietly added files for AI agents to every store it hosts.

The pitch sounds complete: your platform now handles AI for you. Some of that is real, and you should use what you are already paying for. But "the platform added a feature" and "AI assistants can describe your business accurately" are two different claims. Here is where the line falls.

What the free tools cover

  • An introduction file for AI visitors. Most major platforms now generate a file called llms.txt automatically. It gives automated visitors a short, organized introduction to your site, so they can find the pages that matter without guessing.
  • Basic structured facts. Platforms add behind-the-scenes labels, called structured data, that state your business name, type, and contact details in a format machines read easily.
  • A visibility score, on some platforms. Dashboards from providers like Wix and GoDaddy show a score for how visible your site may be to AI tools, based on checks run inside that platform.

These are genuinely useful defaults. A business that had none of this a year ago possibly has some of it today without lifting a finger.

What they miss

Start with a simple limitation: the tools work from whatever happens to be inside your website builder. If a field was left blank when the site was set up, or the details were typed in once and never revisited, the platform faithfully publishes that version.

The gap shows up in the numbers. In an October 2026 scan of 1,902 local business websites, about a quarter already had the AI introduction file, and most of those files were generated by the platform. Yet 45.8 percent of the sites had no structured data describing the business at all. The file layer is arriving quickly. The fact layer is lagging behind it.

  • They do not verify your facts. A platform cannot know that your Saturday hours changed in March. It publishes the version it was given, and an assistant reading that version could quote hours you no longer keep.
  • They cover one website only. Assistants also draw on your Google Business Profile, your directory listings, and your reviews. No website platform manages those surfaces for you.
  • They do not write the substance. Clear service descriptions, a stated service area, and answers to the questions customers ask most are what assistants quote. Defaults do not draft that content.
  • They do not tell you what changed. A dashboard only helps an owner who opens it. Nothing in the free tier watches month to month and flags a problem before a customer runs into it.

A starting point, not a finish

Use the free tools. Then do the part they cannot do for you: open the introduction file your platform generated and read what it says about your business. Check that your services, hours, service area, and contact details are stated, current, and the same everywhere an assistant might look. Where the facts are thin or missing, that is the work that remains.

The platforms have made the first step cheaper, possibly much cheaper. The verification step is still yours, and it is the step that decides whether an assistant describes the business you run today or the one you ran when the site was built.
