Your business information drifts. AI keeps quoting the old version.

October 17, 2026 · By Agent Readiness HQ

Think about everything that has changed at your business in the last year. A price went up. Saturday hours got shorter. You stopped offering one service and started offering another. Each change was small, and each one was probably updated in exactly one place: wherever you happened to be standing when you made it.

Meanwhile, the old version of your business is still out there, on pages and in listings you have not opened in months. And increasingly, that old version is the one doing the talking.

Drift is silent

Nothing notifies you when information goes stale. The outdated hours sit in a directory listing. Last year's price stays on a page nobody visits. A service you dropped might still be described in detail on your own website, because the paragraph was written years ago and never revisited.

AI assistants assemble their answers from this public record, the fresh parts and the stale parts alike. When sources conflict, an assistant could quote the wrong hours, state a price you no longer charge, or describe a service you would have to turn down. The customer hears it stated with confidence, and you may never learn that it happened.

It usually looks like this. A roofing company drops its gutter service, but the old services page still describes it, so an assistant keeps naming gutters when homeowners ask. A salon moves its closing time from seven to six, updates the sign on the door and its Google listing, and forgets the page on its own site that still says walk-ins until seven. Nobody made a careless mistake. The business simply changed in more places than anyone was tracking.

Platforms drift too

It is not only your facts that move. Platforms change how pages are exposed to AI systems, assistants are updated and retrained, and a setup that machines read cleanly in January may read differently by summer. A one-time cleanup captures a moment. It does not hold that moment in place.

That is why a site can be genuinely well prepared in the spring and quietly out of date by fall, with no single moment where anything visibly broke.

The monthly check that catches it

Drift is a maintenance problem, and it has a maintenance answer: a short, regular comparison of what you actually do against what the public record says about you.

  • Keep your current facts in one place: services, hours, prices, service area, and contact details.
  • Compare them against your website and your main listings, and flag every mismatch, however small.
  • Ask an assistant a few customer questions and read its answers about your business for factual errors.
  • Fix the mismatches while they are still small, and repeat the check next month.

None of this is technically hard. The hard part is that it has to keep happening, in a business where the owner is also the crew.

That is the honest case for treating AI readiness as upkeep rather than a project. The cleanup matters. So does everything that happens after it.
