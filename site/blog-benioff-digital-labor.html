Salesforce's CEO says your next hire might not be human

October 2, 2026

At the World Economic Forum in Davos in January 2025, Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff made a prediction that has not stopped echoing. "We are the last CEOs who are only going to be managing humans as our workforce," he said. "From this point forward, we will be managing not only human workers but also digital workers."

Benioff calls it digital labor: AI agents that answer customer questions, qualify leads, and handle work that used to require a person at a desk. He has put Salesforce's money behind the claim with Agentforce, the company's agent platform, and has repeated the line since, including on earnings calls: every CEO going forward, he says, will manage humans and agents together.

The flip side nobody mentions

Most coverage treats digital labor as something that happens inside big companies. But a workforce of agents does not stay inside the building. Those same agents get sent outward: to research vendors, compare options, gather pricing, and report back to the humans they work for.

When that happens, your business is on the other side of the interaction. The "worker" evaluating you is a machine reading your website, looking for clear facts it can verify: what you do, where you work, what it costs, how to reach you. If your site does not give it those answers in a form it can parse, the agent does not call you to clarify. It moves to the next business on the list.

What to do about it

You do not need to hire digital labor to be affected by it. You need your website to hold up when a digital worker comes to check you out: accurate, consistent, machine-readable facts about your business, on pages an agent can actually access. That is a presentation problem, not a rebuild, and it is fixable.
