Yelp is charging AI agents for access. Your website is the way around it.

October 18, 2026

Yelp has confirmed to TechCrunch that AI agents only get access to its listings if the company behind the agent pays for a data license. No license, no access. An agent working for one of your customers cannot pull a quote, join a waitlist, or book through Yelp unless its maker has paid up. (Coverage of the standoff.)

Yelp is not alone. Amazon recently blocked an AI shopping agent from its store. Delta is keeping agents from booking flights. Banks are restricting agent access to accounts. The pattern is the same everywhere: the big platforms are deciding which AI agents get in, and on what terms.

Why this matters for a local service business

If you run a plumbing, roofing, or HVAC company, a lot of your visibility lives on platforms you do not control. When a customer asks an AI assistant to find and compare local businesses, the assistant can only use sources it can actually reach. If a platform has gated that agent out, your listing there might as well not exist for that customer.

Which agents have paid which platforms is a negotiation you are not part of. It will keep shifting, platform by platform, deal by deal. Building your visibility on top of it means building on ground someone else controls.

The one source nobody can gate

Your own website. You decide who can read it. An agent that reaches your site directly can pull your services, hours, service area, and contact details straight from the source, with no license deal in between. That has always been the safer place for your business facts to live. This week it became the essential one.

What to do about it

  • Make sure your site is not blocking AI crawlers. A settings file called robots.txt controls this, and many sites block agents by accident.
  • Put your core facts on your own pages in plain, readable form: services, hours, service area, phone number, how to reach you.
  • Add structured data and an AI Information Page, one clear page of verified facts about your business, so an agent does not have to guess.
  • Keep those facts current. An agent that finds stale hours on your own site has nowhere better to check.
