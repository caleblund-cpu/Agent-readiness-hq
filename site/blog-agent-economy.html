The attention economy is ending. The agent economy is here.

October 18, 2026

For twenty years, the internet ran on attention. Customers found businesses by searching, clicking, and browsing, so businesses competed to be seen: the ad at the top, the listing with the most reviews, the homepage designed to catch an eye in two seconds. Win the click, win the customer. That was the attention economy.

It is not how the next customer arrives. More and more, people hand the searching to an AI agent. The agent reads, compares, and shortlists on the customer's behalf, then presents a recommendation. The customer may never see your ad, your photos, or your homepage at all. That is the agent economy, and it rewards a different thing.

Agents do not browse like people

An AI agent has no attention to win. It does not get tired, distracted, or charmed by a nice color palette. It looks for facts it can verify: what you do, where you do it, when you are open, how to reach you, what it costs. It reads your website the way an auditor reads a filing, looking for clear statements it can rely on.

If those facts are missing, buried in images, or contradicted from page to page, the agent does not slow down and squint. It moves on to a business it can understand. Being ignored by an agent does not feel like losing a click. It feels like nothing at all, which is what makes it dangerous.

What changes for a local business

  • Being understood starts to matter more than being noticed. Pretty still helps with people. It does nothing for a machine reading your facts.
  • Your own website becomes the source of record. Platforms will keep changing their rules about which agents get in. Your site answers to you.
  • Consistency becomes a ranking factor you control. The same hours, services, and service area, stated the same way everywhere an agent can check.
  • The first impression is a paragraph, not a picture. What an agent repeats to your customer is a summary of your facts. Make sure the facts are worth repeating.

How to compete in the agent economy

Start by finding out what an agent sees today. Can it reach your site at all? Can it extract your services, hours, and contact details without guessing? Is there structured data and one clear page of verified facts, an AI Information Page, that states plainly what your business is?

None of this replaces good service or a good reputation. It decides whether the agent doing the shortlisting ever gets far enough to find out about them. The attention economy asked: can they see you? The agent economy asks a harder question: can they understand you?
