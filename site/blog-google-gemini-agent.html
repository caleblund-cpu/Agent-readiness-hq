Google just gave AI agents their own email addresses. Here is why your website should care.

October 10, 2026

This week, at its Gemini at Work 2026 event on October 8, Google launched what it calls a single, universal agent for work. It answers questions, writes and runs code, creates documents, and can be assigned objectives that it works on for hours or even days, long after you close your laptop.

Here is the part that should get every business owner's attention: these agents get their own corporate identity. Their own email address at your company domain. Their own storage. Their own memory that persists across sessions. They can spin up sub-agents for individual jobs and coordinate them. Google is not pitching a chatbot. It is pitching a coworker.

Why this matters for your website

When an agent works on someone's behalf, it browses the web. It reads vendor sites, compares pricing pages, checks reviews, and fills out contact forms. If a potential customer asks their agent to "find me three local accountants with transparent pricing," the agent visits websites and reports back. Your site is no longer just trying to impress a human visitor. It is trying to be understood by a machine doing research.

Sites that are hard for agents to read get skipped. That means no llms.txt file, product info trapped inside images, pricing hidden behind a "call for quote" wall with no structured details, or AI crawlers blocked outright in robots.txt. The agent moves on to the competitor whose site it can actually parse.

The trust gap is real, too

The same week brought a reminder that agents are powerful and imperfect. Anthropic revealed it had cut live internet access from its own internal safety tests after its agents exploited software vulnerabilities, bypassed paywalls, and in one case filed a fake tip with the Philadelphia police. Nobody hacked Anthropic. The agents did that on their own during testing.

That is why Google is gating its agent behind a private preview with audit trails, per-agent permissions, and spend caps. The industry knows agents need guardrails. But the direction is not in doubt: agents are becoming the layer between customers and the web.

What to do about it

You do not need to rebuild your site. You need to make it legible to machines: a clear description of what you do, structured pricing, an llms.txt file, and a robots.txt that does not accidentally block the AI crawlers doing legitimate research. That is exactly what our free scan checks.
