What is llms.txt and does your website need one?

October 10, 2026

You have probably heard of robots.txt, the file that tells search engines which parts of your site to crawl. Now there is a new file with a similar job, but a different audience: AI models. It is called llms.txt, and it lives at the root of your website, at yoursite.com/llms.txt.

What does it do?

When an AI assistant tries to learn about your business, it looks for structured, trustworthy information. Your homepage was written for humans, full of marketing language and design. The llms.txt file is the plain-spoken version: who you are, what you sell, what it costs, and how to contact you. Think of it as an executive summary of your business, written for machines.

What goes in it?

  • Your business name, description, and location
  • Your services or products, in plain language
  • Pricing or pricing ranges
  • Hours, phone number, and contact details
  • Links to your most important pages

Keep it short and factual. This is not a sales page. It is a reference document.

Does your site need one?

If customers might ask an AI assistant for a business like yours, it is worth considering. llms.txt is experimental: a proposed convention intended to provide AI-oriented information about a website, and adoption and support vary. It does not guarantee that any AI system will read or use the file. A well-written file gives automated systems something accurate to work with where supported; without one, systems work from whatever public content they can access.

How do you add one?

On WordPress or a custom-built site, upload a text file named llms.txt to your root folder. That is the whole job. On hosted platforms like Kajabi, Squarespace, or Wix, you cannot upload root files, so you need a workaround, such as serving the file from the edge or building an equivalent information page. Either way, the content matters more than the method.
