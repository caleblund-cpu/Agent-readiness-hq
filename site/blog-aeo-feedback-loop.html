The AEO Feedback Loop: How AI Answers About Your Business Improve Themselves, or Don't

November 10, 2026 · By Agent Readiness HQ

Ask an AI assistant to recommend a business like yours, and it gives you an answer. That answer feels instant, almost like an opinion. It is not an opinion. It is a summary, built on the spot from whatever public information the assistant can find and trust.

Where that information is strong, the answer tends to be strong. Where it is thin, old, or contradictory, the answer may be weak, or your business may not appear in it at all. And here is the part most owners miss: whichever way it goes, it tends to keep going that way. That is the feedback loop.

Where an AI answer actually comes from

An assistant answering "who is a good wedding photographer in Denver" is drawing on a handful of source types:

  • Your own website, if it can be read clearly
  • Directory and listing pages, like Google, Yelp, and industry directories
  • Roundup and "best of" articles written by third parties
  • Reviews, and the patterns across them
  • Pages on other sites that mention or describe your business

Different assistants weight these sources differently. That alone explains a puzzle many owners run into: being named by one assistant and completely absent from another, on the same question, on the same day.

A small test that shows the loop clearly

On November 26, 2026, we asked two AI assistants the same question about documentary style wedding photographers in Denver, Colorado.

One assistant named a local solo photographer first in its answer and cited her own website among its sources. The other assistant named twelve photographers, and she was not one of them. Its answer was built mostly from list and directory pages, and she simply was not prominent enough in those sources to make the cut.

Nothing about her work differs between those two answers. She is the same photographer, with the same portfolio and the same reviews. What differed was the material each assistant happened to build from. Where her own site was part of the foundation, she led the answer. Where the foundation was third party lists, she vanished.

How the loop runs in the wrong direction

Suppose your website states your prices in one place, an old directory states them differently, and a roundup article about your category does not mention you at all. An assistant working from that material could describe you inaccurately, or leave you out to avoid the contradiction.

Now the loop tightens. Customers who never see your name never visit your site, so nothing new gets published about you. The thin sources stay thin. The next answer, built from the same material, possibly looks much like the last one. Nobody made a decision against your business. The record just never improved, so the answers never did.

How it runs in the other direction

The same loop can work for you. When the underlying sources are clear and consistent, an assistant can describe your business with more confidence. It may name you, cite your pages, and state your facts correctly. That visibility could lead to more visits, more reviews, and more mentions, which become part of the record the next answer is built from.

None of this is guaranteed, and it does not happen on a schedule. But the direction of the loop is set by the quality of the sources underneath it, and several of those sources are yours to improve.

What an owner can actually do

  • Fix your own foundation first. Your website should state your services, service area, pricing, and policies in plain text a machine can read, the same way on every page.
  • Keep listings consistent. The facts on your directory profiles should match your website. Contradictions are a common reason an assistant hedges or moves on.
  • Be present where answers are built. Roundups, directories, and review profiles in your category are not just marketing. They are raw material for AI answers, and being absent from them could mean being absent from the answers too.
  • Check the answers themselves, not just your site. A website can be perfectly readable while the answers built from other sources still leave you out. That is why we check actual AI answers on a recurring basis for the businesses we monitor, rather than assuming a good site settles the question.

The loop is already running

Whether you pay attention to it or not, AI assistants are answering questions about your category today, from the sources that exist today. The only real choice is whether the material those answers are built from gets better over time, or stays exactly as it is.
