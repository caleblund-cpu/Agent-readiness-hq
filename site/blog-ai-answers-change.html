Ask an AI about your business twice. You may get two different answers.

October 24, 2026 · By Agent Readiness HQ

Try a small experiment. Open the AI assistant of your choice and ask it a question a customer might ask: "Who is a good plumber near me that handles water heaters?" Note which businesses it names. Then ask again tomorrow, or rephrase the question slightly, and compare the two answers.

The lists may not match. A business named first today could be third tomorrow, or missing entirely. If you have seen this happen and wondered whether you did something wrong, you probably did not. The answers themselves move.

It is not just you

In one 2026 study, researchers had hundreds of people ask AI assistants the same questions repeatedly, then compared thousands of answers side by side. The exact same list of businesses came back less than 1 percent of the time. The same list in the same order was rarer still.

That is not a defect in any one tool. It is how these systems work. Each answer is assembled on the spot from a large and shifting pool of information, and small differences in timing, wording, or the sources consulted can change which businesses get named.

Why the answers move

A few forces are at work:

  • The assistants themselves are updated regularly, sometimes in ways that change how they weigh one source against another.
  • The public record changes too. A competitor updates a page, a new review appears, a directory refreshes its data, and the next answer potentially draws on different material.
  • The question matters. "Plumber near me" and "emergency plumber open now" are different questions, and an assistant may answer them from different facts.

None of this makes AI answers useless. It makes any single answer a sample, not a verdict.

What this means for anyone selling you a ranking

If a service promises to get you ranked number one in AI answers, or quotes you an exact AI ranking position, be skeptical. Nobody controls which businesses an assistant names on a given day, and a position measured once might not exist an hour later.

The same caution applies to a score built on a single check. A snapshot shows what one answer looked like on one afternoon. It cannot tell you whether your business is becoming easier or harder for assistants to find and describe as the months go by.

What to track instead

Trends. Ask a wide set of real customer questions, phrased the way customers actually phrase them, and repeat the exercise on a regular schedule. Appearing once means little. Appearing in a growing share of answers across several months could mean your information is getting easier for assistants to find and use.

Facts. Whether you appear is only half the question. When an assistant does describe your business, are the hours right? The phone number? The services? A factual error deserves more attention than a position change, because it is concrete, it is checkable, and it can be fixed.

Consistency. Assistants build answers from your website, your listings, and other public sources. When those sources agree with each other, an assistant can describe your business with more confidence. When they conflict, it may hedge, guess, or leave you out entirely.

The practical takeaway

You cannot freeze an AI answer in place, and you do not need to. Keep your facts accurate and consistent everywhere they appear, check periodically how assistants are describing you, and judge the direction over months rather than the outcome on any single day.
