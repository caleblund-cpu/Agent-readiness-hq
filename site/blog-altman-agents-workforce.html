Sam Altman called it in January 2025: agents are joining the workforce

September 24, 2026

On January 6, 2025, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman published a short post called "Reflections." One sentence in it aged fast: "We believe that, in 2025, we may see the first AI agents 'join the workforce' and materially change the output of companies."

He was right, and then some. Agents now book travel, place orders, answer support queues, and run research errands for the people who employ them. Nvidia's Jensen Huang was saying the same thing around the same time, calling enterprise adoption of agentic AI "the latest rage" on an earnings call. The people building the technology were not hedging about where it was going.

A workforce that shops, compares, and reports back

Here is the part that matters for a business like yours. An agent in the workforce does not just do internal chores. When its employer needs a service, the agent goes looking. It searches, visits websites, compares what it can verify, and brings back a shortlist with reasons.

That process is brutally literal. The agent can only shortlist businesses it can understand. A site with clear services, a clear service area, and facts stated in plain text gives the agent something to work with. A site where the important details are vague, outdated, or trapped in formats machines struggle with gives it nothing, and nothing is what your business becomes in its report.

What to do about it

Altman's agents work for their employers, not for you. You cannot pitch them. You can only be legible to them. The good news is that legibility is mostly housekeeping: say what you do, say where you do it, keep your facts consistent everywhere they appear, and make sure nothing on your site accidentally blocks the agents doing the looking.
