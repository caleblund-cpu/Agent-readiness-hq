SEO for the Agent Economy: When Your Next Customer Is a Machine

November 24, 2026 · By Agent Readiness HQ

For most of the internet's history, your customer was a person with a browser. They searched, clicked, skimmed your homepage, and decided in a few seconds whether to call. Websites were built for that person, and search engine optimization, SEO, was about winning their click.

A new kind of visitor is arriving. Increasingly, the first researcher your business meets is not the customer at all. It is an AI agent, a program the customer has sent to do the legwork: gather options, compare them, check prices and policies, and come back with a shortlist. The customer may only ever see the agent's summary. Your website's real first impression is increasingly made on a machine.

What an agent does differently

An agent does not browse the way a person does. It does not admire your photography or get a feeling from your homepage. It reads, literally and quickly, looking for facts it can rely on: what you offer, where you offer it, what it costs, what your policies are, and how to reach you.

If those facts are easy to extract, the agent can describe your business accurately and possibly include it in the shortlist. If they are missing, locked inside images, or contradicted from page to page, the agent does not call to clarify. It moves on to a business it can understand. From your side, that loss is silent. No click, no call, no sign it ever happened.

A sign of where this is heading

On November 25, 2026, Cloudflare announced a feature called Search for Agents. Cloudflare runs the infrastructure behind a large share of the world's websites. The feature lets a site offer a search interface built for AI agents: instead of scraping the site's pages and hoping to make sense of them, an agent can ask the site questions directly and get clean, structured answers from its content.

That is a meaningful shift in how the web expects to be read. The biggest plumbing company in the room is now building for a web where machines query businesses directly, the way people use a search box today.

The honest limits, today

It is worth being straight about the current state of this. Search for Agents is a Cloudflare feature, available to sites hosted on Cloudflare. Most small businesses are not. Their websites run on platforms like Squarespace, Wix, or GoDaddy, and those platforms do not offer an agent search interface today. Nobody is going to lose a customer this month for lacking one.

So treat this as direction of travel, not a requirement. The direction is clear enough: businesses will increasingly be evaluated by machines before they are ever seen by people. The practical question for a small business is not how to install an agent API. It is whether the basics an agent needs are in order on the platforms that exist now.

What to do on the web we actually have

The steps that help today are unglamorous, and they help with human visitors and search engines too:

  • Keep one clear page of facts. Services, service area, pricing, and policies, stated plainly in text. An agent that finds this page can understand your business in one pass.
  • Add structured data. A small block of code that labels your facts for machines, so nothing important depends on an agent interpreting your marketing copy correctly.
  • Publish an llms.txt file. A short summary of your business written for AI systems. It is the closest thing most platforms allow today to introducing yourself to an agent directly, and it could become more important as agent traffic grows.
  • Say the same thing everywhere. Your site, your Google profile, and your listings should agree. Consistency is what lets a machine state your facts with confidence instead of quietly dropping them.

None of this requires rebuilding your website. It is a layer underneath the site you already have, and it is the layer agents actually read.

The short version

Your next customer may still be a person. But the researcher who builds their shortlist could be a machine, and that researcher judges you entirely on whether your facts can be found, read, and trusted. Businesses that make that easy may find themselves recommended more often. Businesses that do not may never learn what they missed.
